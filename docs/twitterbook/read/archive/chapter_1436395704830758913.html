I seem to have symmetric recovery needs. If I work an hour I need an hour to unwind. 4 hours on, 4 hours to unwind. 1 day work, 1 day to unwind, 2 days on, 2 days to unwind. The pattern breaks around 5 days, where recovery is 2 days (and not just because of weekends)
🔗
But I can’t do multiple weeks in a row “on” with a 5-2 cycle. Every 3-4 weeks I need a whole week off. Every few months, I need a whole month almost off.

If I were the optimizing type (I’m not) I’d figure out the max output duty cycle based on efficient recovery sweet spots.
🔗
I think if you average properly across on/recovery periods across all scales, most of us can really on,y do 55-60% duty cycles. So the American model of 5-2 weekly, plus 2 weeks vacation plus a week of other holidays and sick days works out to near optimal I suspect.

There’s more than fundamental attribution error going on with people who think they’re special. They misunderstand the very mechanics of problem solving. They think their solution is 80% of anyone else’s and their specialness lies in seeing what nobody else has.
🔗
It’s more like they have 20%, and the part that’s new is not good and the part that’s good is not new and probably rediscovered routinely by half the population every generation.

But a person who thinks they’re ordinary instinctively starts with these assumptions.

I shouldn’t be so mean to waldenponders and deepworkers. They’re more wrong for reasons they can’t control than malicious. Navigating life with a persistent and perhaps repressed sense of your own current specialness and future significance must be really hard.
