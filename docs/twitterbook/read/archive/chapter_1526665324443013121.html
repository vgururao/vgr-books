I do think it’s a peculiar artifact of our times. When sustaining life is increasingly a SaaS service configuration type problem and you spend more and more time navigating customer service escalations, all reflections are likely to be “review” type thoughts
🔗
Almost the entire human environment exists to serve you in some way, and any unhappiness you experience is someone’s job to address, even if they generally do it poorly. You have to really go out of your way to experience realities where your unhappiness is nobody’s job.
🔗
Even fairly abstract things. Like say you buy into startup hustle culture and try and fail at startups. Obviously it is Paul Graham’s fault for providing a less than 5 stars playbook and service. Write a review!

Meh lunar eclipse? Yelp review the astronomy-event hypebeasts.
🔗
I’m genuinely wondering now… is there any life experience you can’t process into a Yelp review of a person or institution that packages and serves it up under expectations set by terms of service that they might fail to meet
🔗
On the flip side can you live an unreviewable life? I think so. I suspect I come close personally 😎
