1/ Tweetstorm: What's The Problem With The Last Block?
🔗
2/ In all sorts of network logistics (transport, telegraph, telephone etc), historically the "last mile" has been the bane of infrastructure
🔗
3/ The last mile was hard between 1970-1980 mainly because of physical complexity and legal right-of-way complexity
🔗
4/ In raw numbers, the last mile probably accounts for the bulk of actual miles traveled by anything on a network due to number of endpoints
🔗
5/ The last mile is the last to go hi-tech. Containerization stops at city limits. Fiber optics shifted to local-loop copper until recently
🔗
6/ The last block though, is hard for other reasons. It's still physically and legally complex, but that isn't the hardest part anymore.
🔗
7/ Two forces make the last block especially hard: increased demand and inequality.
🔗
8/ Increased demand is obvious: USPS/FedEx etc weren't built with this much package flow in mind. Neither were front porches.
🔗
9/ Inequality is less obvious: in an unequal society there is more incentive for low-level theft and pilfering, easiest at the last block.
🔗
10/ I don't have numbers, but anecdotally, theft from porches etc. has risen: more temptation, more people in state to be tempted.
🔗
11/ Concierge services for accepting packages are now increasingly a necessity in bigger cities.
🔗
12/ You have both large, high-value packages that are awkward for small lockers, stairwells, and jewelry-level value in things like iPhones
🔗
13/ The last block is also undergoing weird transformation on other networks. Consider net metering in solar power, charging of Teslas etc.
🔗
14/ For eg. there are now fights over charging in charging stations, and homeowners setting up informal charging services on lawns.
🔗
15/ Even more subtleties: Airbnb etc. require more sophisticated security for the last block: key transfers, digital locks etc.
🔗
16/ And as more and more value in flow (VIF?) is in the last block at any given time, incentives for conflict and crime increase.
🔗
17/ "Stealing" cable or electricity required some sophistication, "stealing" wifi is both easier and more valuable.
🔗
18/ Last block also is economy in its own right: from unlocking smart-contract rental cars to power outlets in cafes that charge for charge
🔗
19/ A lot is low-value/high volume so online micropayments arguments ("just make it free"/"not worth financializing") apply. But not all.
🔗
10/ I'm going to shrug off pennies worth of electric power or wifi used for free as community goodwill. Stolen package, not so much.
🔗
21/ F. Pohl said "job of the sci-fi writer is to predict not the automobile but the traffic jam." Traffic jam is ALWAYS at leaves of tree
🔗
22/ Literal traffic jams happen near cities. As s/w eats a network-provisioned service, traffic jam moves further down into capillaries.
🔗
23/ something something information in black hole is at surface, holographic principle
🔗
24/ I once pitched feature on last-mile to a mag; editor rejected it as too esoteric :D But if you get tech, it's where ALL action is
🔗
25/ With VR, NFC, payments... It's not even the last block or last yard by your doorstep, it's the last inch from your face wherever you are
🔗
26/ General topological theory why this happens. A more informationally powerful tech induces a higher resolution network structure
🔗
27/ Resolution "delta" (tens of miles for trains/planes, miles for cars, blocks for foot/electricity, inches for wireless) extends network
🔗
28/ Network core can be defined as the "low res" backbonet where economics allow aggregation leverage, and low transaction costs for $ flow
🔗
29/ Every tech stops being organized by econ, starts being organized by social norms at its transaction resolution limit, set by tx costs
🔗
30/ So sociologically, the last mile/block/inch is where the market stops and priceless values etc. start to kick in.
🔗
31/ When large scale disruption happens due to a major tech like s/w, social-norms space gets systematically pushed back by market space.
🔗
32/ Physics: this is tendency towards "plenty of room at the bottom" (Feynman). As market occupies that room, sociology yields to economics
🔗
33/ Transient is ugly because you're shifting regimes, converting social capital into financial capital, hurting social-capital-rich types
🔗
34/ But if you think this is almost over, think again. It's just beginning. The next place to get gentrified? Inches away from your nose.
🔗
35/ You know how they say, "your freedom of action ends where my nose begins"... about to get pretty literal.
🔗
36/ Overall though, you never were that free too far from your face. You're just substituting market for tyranny of neighbors' norms for
🔗
