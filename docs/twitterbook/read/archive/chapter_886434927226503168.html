2/ Apocalyptic fiction loves the theme of some mysterious X that destroys digital tech and bumps us back to bows and arrows
🔗
3/ But digital is not necessarily "high tech" or silicon based
🔗
4/ Neal Stephenson has a complex encryption algorithm using playing cards in cryptonomicon. Morse code is digital.
🔗
5/ So taking "digital" broadly, you can imagine it in embodied in ever medium from abacus to paper to semaphore to telegraph to silicon
🔗
6/ Let's define "post-apocalyptic" as a) loss of deep industrial stack tech (no VHDL, fabs, b) loss of broad global social connectivity
🔗
7/ What digital opportunities might exist? Is digital even worth anything without silicon tech and Moore's law?
🔗
8/ Opportunity #1: Build purely mechanical, steampunk computers a la Babbage/Hollerith/looms BUT with modern theoretical knowledge
🔗
9/ Opportunity #2: Build a mechanical semaphore network, visible to all, that uses packet routing (old semaphores were circuit-switched)
🔗
10/ Opportunity #3: Clock signals are central to digital technology. Develop square wave generators using mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatics
🔗
11/ In the examples above I've avoided electricity. If you can do electricity, you're not in a post-apocalyptic world. Just a shitty one.
