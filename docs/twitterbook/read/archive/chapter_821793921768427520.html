“"Good for business" and "bad for business" look VERY different depending on your…”
🔗
"Good for business" and "bad for business" look VERY different depending on your current scale and cash position.
🔗
1/ I want to sketch out a difference between two kinds of 'good for business' environments that people usually don't appreciate
🔗
2/ A central fact of free-market capitalism is that capital does not care HOW it gains a return, and has no natural "time horizon"
🔗
3/ Like evolution, capital-seeking-returns is a blind and means-agnostic process. The sole justification for what works is that it works
🔗
4/ It takes a human investor to care about a) how a return is generated b) over what time period it is generated
🔗
5/ There are two kinds of "good for business" environments, which I think of as "stacked for profiteering" and "stacked for innovation"
🔗
6/ In stacked-for-profiteering environment, main asset you need to do business is large pile of concentrated capital. Bigger is better. Why?
🔗
7/ To jumpstart very rapid economic performance you need BIG numbers quickly. It takes a big bump to create growth on a $13 trillion base.
🔗
8/ That's why the go-to is energy (gas, oil, coal) and dismantling public sector institutions (allowing a "prime contractor" economy)
🔗
9/ A stacked-for-profiteering economy isn't growing wealth. It is moving it around in very large chunks, creating illusion of wealth-making
🔗
10/ The logic is mercantilist, zero-sum, even if it doesn't have a legible basis like land or mineral rights.
🔗
11/ But there is another kind of good-for-business: stacked for innovation. You need some capital, but more capital is not linearly "better"
🔗
12/ As modern startup tropes go, you don't even need 2 guys in garage with circuit boards anymore. 1 guy with a laptop at starbucks will do.
🔗
13/ What you do need is safe, liberal environment that rewards curiosity, allows people with diverse identities to live cheaply, together
🔗
14/ Stacked-for-profiteering needs high financial capital, low cultural capital (homogeneous rich crowd at Davos is enough to make it work)
🔗
15/ Stacked-for-innovation needs low-medium financial capital, high cultural capital, primarily sustained by public infrastructure
🔗
16/ Public infrastructure/capital is inefficient in a narrow sense, and Davos ideologues obsessively focus on the inefficiency above all
🔗
17/ But in a broader sense, what you get in return for capital inefficiency in a narrow sense is inclusive social-capital catalysis
🔗
18/ It is no accident that under siege, one of the first instincts of "elitist" cities is to look for ways to become immigrant refuges
🔗
19/ Coastal American cities have served as innovation-business centers in part because they offer refuge, ESPECIALLY to domestic immigrants
🔗
20/ So Trumpism may be good for biz in Davos/DC profiteering/cronyism sense that is agnostic to trade wars. Those weeds thrive at all times
🔗
21/ That kind of good-for-business drives the "bad" kinds of bubble-bust cycles of mercantilist extraction and exproporiation/capital flight
🔗
22/ But if you want good-for-business in the innovation sense, preserve liberal, safe cities or you're dead, despite any short-term bumps
🔗
23/ In short, all the mindless empty-suit types who are falling in line behind the "good for business" narrative are basically idiots
🔗
24/ They have never seen "good for business" economy that is 100% profiteering 0% innovation. They don't know what they will lose.
🔗
25/ You want efficient, free-market, booming-business dystopia? Full of commodities merchants of 16th century type? Oil/gas/coal "growth"?
🔗
26/ Then get behind this bullshit narrative. If not, get real. No social liberalism, no innovation, the "business" you care about dying.
