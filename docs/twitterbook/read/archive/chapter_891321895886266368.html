“1/ Let's define civilization as as an aspirational state where there is no need …”
🔗
1/ Let's define civilization as as an aspirational state where there is no need to intentionally cause pain to others to get what you want
🔗
2/ If "good" is defined as people generally on board with civilizing process (ie don't want to cause needless pain), what % of us are good?
🔗
3/ In answering this q we tend to replace moral absolutes of good/evil with shades of grey, to acknowledge complexity of trying to be good
🔗
4/ Ie we assume that most people try to be good in our sense, but complexity and moral ambiguities of world defeats them. Is this true?
🔗
5/ Alternate theory: being good is not as hard as we make it out to be, the grey areas aren't that grey.
🔗
6/ What if people AREN'T mostly good in the sense of not wanting to cause pain? What if causing pain in central to lives of many?
🔗
7/ I call this "shades of red" theory as opposed to shades of grey. To varying degrees a lot more people want to cause unnecessary pain
🔗
8/ Here is a picture I made of 'shades of red" theory
🔗
9/ We are raised to believe only small % ("criminals") want to cause pain for gain. Most don't. A few saints suffer for others (top left)
🔗
10/ Shades of red theory asserts that majority - everybody to right of "apathetic" - gets off on others' unnecessary pain at least a little
🔗
11/ Shades of red is based on a deontological notion of evil -- finding pleasure in causing/witnessing pain. Good is negatively defined.
🔗
12/ By this theory, most "good" people are actual bad: they define "evil" to dehumanize, to justify causing pain for pleasure
🔗
13/ Our penal system implicitly assumes shades-of-red theory. Prison guards and cops I suspect have at least a small streak of sadism.
🔗
14/ Stanford prison experiments: almost all of us are capable of finding pleasure in causing/witnessing pain
🔗
15/ If this is true, my original definition of civilizing project is nonsensical and most people are NOT on board with it
🔗
16/ Worse, most moral philosophy is obsessed with defining evil primarily to find targets for justifiable pain-causing.
🔗
17/ Enemies give life meaning and purpose. Moralizing is overtly about trying to do good, but covertly about prepping victims for pain
🔗
18/ The most common form is NIMBYism/tribalism. For many, the ingroup is formed in order to create an outgroup it's ok to hate and hurt
🔗
19/ As a mostly-apathetic, I only came very reluctantly to this conclusion: that many actively enjoy causing/witnessing pain
🔗
20/ It's an ugly theory of humans, but unfortunately very plausible evolutionarily. Finding pleasure in pain is adaptive in scarcity.
🔗
21/ Note that feeling others' pain (friends, kin, ingroup, random others) is not mutually exclusive with finding pleasure in pain
🔗
22/ You might be capable of both. But people incapable of finding pleasure in others' pain might be minority mutants.
🔗
23/ Which means the feel-good theory of civilization I opened with is a theory of, by, for freaks who can't find pleasure in others' pain
🔗
24/ Which brings us to the dark question: if finding pleasure in others' pain is central to psyche of "normal" humans, what is civilization?
🔗
25/ It might very well be that a scalable, meaningful utopia for most humans will be based on bloodsports and torturable AIs in VR 😐
🔗
